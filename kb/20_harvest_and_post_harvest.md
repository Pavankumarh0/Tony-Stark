# Sowing, Harvest and Post-Harvest Practices

Review status: general guide compiled for Smart Krishi from standard agronomy references.

## Preparing for sowing
Plough the land after the summer showers to kill weeds and expose pests to the sun. Use certified seed of a variety recommended for your area, from the Raitha Samparka Kendra or a trusted dealer, and keep the bill. Treat seed with a bio-agent such as Trichoderma or with the recommended seed treatment before sowing, to protect against seed and soil borne diseases. Sow at the right time for the season, because late sowing increases pest damage and lowers yield.

## Seed treatment with biofertilizers
Treating seed with Rhizobium for pulses and groundnut, and with Azospirillum for cereals and millets, helps the crop take nitrogen from the air. Phosphate solubilising bacteria make soil phosphorus available to the crop. Treat seed in the shade and sow soon after treatment, because sunlight kills these beneficial bacteria.

## Knowing when to harvest
Harvest at the right maturity: grain crops when the grains are hard and the plants turn yellow or brown, pulses when most pods turn brown, and vegetables when they reach the size and colour the market wants. Harvesting too early gives shrivelled grain; harvesting too late causes shattering, bird damage and loss in the field.

## Drying and threshing grain
Dry harvested grain in the sun on a clean threshing floor or tarpaulin, not on the road or bare soil, until it is hard and rattles. Damp grain gets mould, heats up and is attacked by storage insects. Clean the grain of stones, chaff and broken grains before storage or sale, because clean grain fetches a better price.

## Storing grain and pulses
Store only well dried grain, in clean, dry bags or bins kept off the floor on wooden planks and away from walls. Hermetic bags and metal bins keep out air and storage insects. Neem leaves mixed with stored grain help keep insects away. Check stored grain every few weeks for insects, mould and rats. Pulses store better when mixed with a small amount of edible oil or kept in airtight containers.

## Handling vegetables and fruits after harvest
Harvest vegetables and fruits in the cool morning hours, keep them in the shade and handle them gently to avoid bruising. Sort out damaged produce, grade by size and pack in ventilated crates rather than sacks. Do not wash produce that is going to be stored. Low cost cool chambers made of bricks and wet sand keep vegetables fresh for a few extra days.

## Adding value to produce
Simple processing raises income: cleaning, grading and packing grain, making flour from millets, drying and powdering chilli, and making pickles. Farmer Producer Organisations help farmers with storage, processing and selling. Government schemes give subsidy for warehouses, cold storage and processing units.

# Crop Climate Needs and Sensor-Based Advice

Review status: general guide compiled for Smart Krishi from standard agronomy references. Varieties and crop stages differ, so check the local package of practices (UAS Bangalore, UAS Dharwad, KVK).

## How Smart Krishi uses the temperature reading
When a farmer asks for the temperature, Smart Krishi gives the air temperature measured by the farm sensor, says whether it suits the farmer's crop, and names the crops that grow best at that temperature. A temperature below a crop's lower limit slows growth and can damage young plants; above its upper limit, flowers and young fruits can drop.

## How Smart Krishi uses the soil moisture reading
For a calibrated soil sensor, a reading below 30 percent means the soil is dry and the crop should be irrigated soon, a reading between 30 and 80 percent is a good range, and a reading above 80 percent means the soil is very wet, so irrigation should stop and the field should drain. These bands are a rough guide: each field has its own dry and wet readings, and the soil should always be checked by hand at root depth before irrigating.

## How Smart Krishi uses the water level reading
The water level sensor shows what percent of its sensor strip is under water. Below 5 percent there is no water at the sensor, below 35 percent the water level is low, below 70 percent it is moderate, and above that it is high. In a tank or farm pond, a low level means it should be refilled before the next irrigation; in a field, a high level means water may be standing, which only rice tolerates.

## Temperature and water needs of rice (paddy)
Rice (paddy) grows best at an air temperature between 25 and 32 degrees Celsius. Below 20 degrees it is too cold and above 35 degrees it is too hot for good growth. Its water need is high. Main season in Karnataka: kharif (June to October); summer crop where water is assured.

Ragi (finger millet) grows best at an air temperature between 26 and 29 degrees Celsius. Below 20 degrees it is too cold and above 35 degrees it is too hot for good growth. Its water need is low. Main season in Karnataka: kharif (June to September sowing).

# Field Crops of Karnataka: How to Grow Them

Review status: general guide compiled for Smart Krishi from standard agronomy references. Durations are typical and change with the variety. Seed rate, spacing and the best variety for your area should come from the local package of practices (UAS Bangalore, UAS Dharwad, UAS Raichur, UAHS Shivamogga) or the Krishi Vigyan Kendra.

## Rice (paddy): season, soil and sowing
Rice is grown mainly in the kharif season, from June to October, and as a summer crop where irrigation water is assured. It grows best in clay or clay loam soils that hold water. Seedlings are raised in a nursery and transplanted when they are about three to four weeks old, or seed is sown directly in puddled or dry fields. Medium duration varieties take about 120 to 150 days and short duration varieties about 100 to 120 days.

## Rice (paddy): water, harvest and problems
Rice needs plenty of water. Keep a shallow layer of water in the field during tillering and flowering, and drain the field about two weeks before harvest. Alternate wetting and drying, where the field is allowed to dry slightly before the next irrigation, saves water without losing yield. Harvest when most grains in the panicle turn golden yellow. Common problems are blast, sheath blight, bacterial leaf blight, stem borer and brown planthopper.

## Ragi (finger millet): season, soil and sowing
Ragi is the main millet of south Karnataka and is grown mainly as a rainfed kharif crop, sown from June to August. It grows on red sandy loam and other light soils and tolerates poor soils better than most crops. It can be sown directly in lines or transplanted from a nursery. Most varieties take about 100 to 120 days. Ragi is nutritious, rich in calcium and fibre, and stores well for years.

## Ragi (finger millet): water, harvest and problems
Ragi needs little water and survives short dry spells, but irrigation at flowering and grain filling improves yield if rains fail. Harvest when the earheads turn brown and the grains are hard. The main disease is blast, which affects leaves, the neck and the fingers of the earhead; resistant varieties and timely sowing reduce it.

## Maize: season, soil and sowing
Maize is grown in kharif and rabi in Karnataka, especially in Davanagere, Haveri, Chitradurga and Belagavi. It grows best in well drained loamy soils and does not tolerate waterlogging. Hybrids take about 100 to 120 days. Sow in lines after the first good rains in kharif.

## Maize: water, harvest and problems
The most critical stages for water are tasselling, silking and grain filling; water stress then causes poorly filled cobs. Harvest when the husk turns dry and brown and the grains are hard. The main problems are fall armyworm, stem borer, turcicum leaf blight and downy mildew.

## Jowar (sorghum): season, soil and sowing
Jowar is grown as a kharif crop in south Karnataka and as a rabi crop on the black soils of north Karnataka, where it grows on moisture stored in the soil after the rains. It tolerates heat and drought. Most varieties take about 100 to 120 days. Rabi jowar is sown in September and October.

## Jowar (sorghum): harvest and problems
Harvest when the grains are hard and the leaves begin to dry. Shoot fly is a serious pest of young plants, so timely sowing is important. Grain mould affects kharif jowar when it rains at harvest. The stalks are valuable dry fodder for cattle.

## Red gram (tur): season, soil and sowing
Red gram is a major pulse of north Karnataka, especially Kalaburagi, Bidar and Vijayapura. It is sown in June and July as a kharif crop, often as an intercrop with jowar, ragi or groundnut. It grows on well drained black and red soils and does not tolerate waterlogging. Most varieties take about 150 to 180 days, and some shorter varieties are available.

## Red gram (tur): care, harvest and problems
As a pulse, red gram fixes nitrogen from the air through its root nodules, so it needs less nitrogen fertilizer and improves the soil for the next crop. Harvest when most pods turn brown. Pod borer is the most serious pest, and wilt and sterility mosaic are the main diseases; resistant varieties help.

## Bengal gram (chickpea): season and care
Bengal gram is a rabi pulse sown in October and November on the black soils of north Karnataka, where it grows on stored soil moisture. It prefers cool, dry weather and is harmed by heavy rain and cloudy weather at flowering. Most varieties take about 90 to 110 days. Nipping the tips of young plants encourages branching. Pod borer and wilt are the main problems.

## Groundnut: season, soil and sowing
Groundnut is grown in kharif and as a summer crop under irrigation, especially in Chitradurga, Tumakuru and Chikkaballapura. It needs light, well drained red sandy loam soil so that the pegs can enter the soil and the pods can form. Most varieties take about 100 to 120 days. Gypsum applied at flowering supplies calcium and sulphur and improves pod filling.

## Groundnut: water, harvest and problems
Groundnut needs water at flowering, pegging and pod development. Harvest when the inner side of the pod shell turns dark and the leaves turn yellow. Dry the pods well before storage to prevent aflatoxin mould. The main problems are tikka leaf spot, rust, leaf miner and white grub.

## Sunflower: season and care
Sunflower is grown in kharif, rabi and summer seasons in north Karnataka and is fairly drought tolerant. Most hybrids take about 90 to 100 days. Bees are needed for good seed set, so avoid spraying insecticides during flowering. Harvest when the back of the head turns yellow to brown. Parrots and other birds damage maturing heads.

## Cotton: season, soil and sowing
Cotton is a kharif crop of north Karnataka, sown from May to July, especially in Raichur, Ballari, Haveri and Dharwad. It grows best on deep black soils that hold moisture. Most hybrids take about 150 to 180 days. It needs a long frost free season with warm days.

## Cotton: care, harvest and problems
Pick cotton in the morning after the dew has dried, when the bolls have opened fully, and keep the lint clean and dry. Pink bollworm, whitefly, jassids and other sucking pests are the main problems. Pheromone traps help to watch pink bollworm, and removing crop residues after harvest breaks its life cycle.

## Sugarcane: season and planting
Sugarcane is grown in Belagavi, Bagalkot, Mandya and Vijayapura. It is planted through the year using setts, which are cut pieces of cane with buds, and takes about 12 to 18 months. It needs a lot of water and deep, fertile, well drained soil. A ratoon crop can be taken from the stubble after harvest.

## Sugarcane: care, harvest and problems
Earth up the crop and remove dry leaves to reduce pests. Drip irrigation saves a large amount of water in sugarcane. Harvest when the cane is mature, as advised by the sugar factory. Early shoot borer, internode borer, woolly aphid and red rot are the main problems.

## Wheat: season and care
Wheat is grown as a rabi crop in north Karnataka, mainly in Belagavi, Dharwad and Vijayapura, sown in October and November. It needs cool weather during growth and warm, dry weather at ripening. Most varieties take about 100 to 120 days. Crown root initiation, about three weeks after sowing, is the most critical stage for irrigation. Rust is the main disease.

## Crop rotation and intercropping
Growing the same crop on the same land every season builds up pests, diseases and weeds and drains the same nutrients. Rotating a cereal such as ragi or maize with a pulse such as red gram or bengal gram improves the soil, because pulses fix nitrogen. Intercropping, such as ragi with red gram or groundnut with red gram, reduces the risk of total crop loss when the rains fail.

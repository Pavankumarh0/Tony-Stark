# Vegetables, Fruits and Plantation Crops: How to Grow Them

Review status: general guide compiled for Smart Krishi from standard horticulture references. Durations are typical and change with the variety. Seed rate, spacing, fertilizer for crops not listed in the fertilizer document, and the best variety for your area should come from the Department of Horticulture, UHS Bagalkot or the Krishi Vigyan Kendra.

## Onion: season and growing
Onion is grown in kharif and rabi, especially in Chitradurga, Vijayapura, Gadag and Bagalkot. It needs well drained loamy soil; it rots in waterlogged soil. Seedlings are raised in a nursery for about six weeks and then transplanted, and the crop is ready about three to four months after transplanting. Stop irrigation about two weeks before harvest so the bulbs mature and store better.

## Onion: harvest and storage
Harvest when most of the leaves bend and fall over. Dry the bulbs in the field and then in shade, a process called curing, until the neck is dry and tight. Store cured bulbs in a well ventilated, dry structure. Thrips and purple blotch are the main problems.

## Chilli: season and growing
Chilli is grown as a kharif crop in Haveri (Byadgi chilli), Dharwad, Ballari and Raichur. Seedlings are raised in a nursery and transplanted after about six weeks. It needs well drained soil and suffers in waterlogging. Green chilli harvests start about two months after transplanting, and red chilli is picked when the fruits turn fully red. Dry red chilli on clean tarpaulins or drying yards, not on bare soil, to avoid fungal contamination.

## Chilli: problems
Leaf curl, which is spread by thrips, mites and whiteflies, is the most serious problem of chilli. Remove plants with curled leaves early, use yellow and blue sticky traps, and grow a border crop of maize or jowar to block the insects. Fruit rot and powdery mildew are other common diseases.

## Potato: season and growing
Potato is grown in the kharif season in Hassan and Chikkamagaluru and in the rabi season in other districts. It needs cool weather and loose, well drained soil. Use healthy, disease free seed tubers from a trusted source, because seed tubers carry diseases. Earthing up covers the tubers and prevents them turning green. Most varieties are harvested about 90 to 110 days after planting. Late blight is the most serious disease.

## Brinjal: growing and problems
Brinjal is grown in all seasons. Seedlings are transplanted about four to six weeks after sowing, and harvesting begins about two months later and continues for several months. Pick fruits while they are tender and shiny. Shoot and fruit borer is the main pest: remove and destroy wilted shoots and bored fruits every week and use pheromone traps.

## Cabbage and cauliflower: growing and problems
Cabbage and cauliflower need cool weather and are grown mainly in the rabi season and in the hill areas. Seedlings are transplanted about four to five weeks after sowing. Harvest cabbage when the head is firm, and cauliflower when the curd is compact and white. Diamondback moth is the main pest; growing a few rows of mustard as a trap crop and using pheromone traps reduce it.

## Beans: growing
French beans and field beans are grown in kharif and rabi. They grow best in well drained soil in mild weather. Pods are picked while tender, starting about two months after sowing for bush types. Beans fix nitrogen from the air, like other pulses.

## Banana: growing and care
Banana is planted through the year using suckers or tissue culture plants, which are disease free. It needs a lot of water and rich, well drained soil, and drip irrigation suits it well. Remove extra suckers so that one or two remain with the mother plant. Prop the plants with bamboo when the bunch forms so they do not fall. The bunch is harvested about 12 to 15 months after planting, depending on the variety. Panama wilt, Sigatoka leaf spot and bunchy top are the main diseases.

## Coconut: care
Coconut is a perennial crop grown along the coast and in Tumakuru, Hassan and Chitradurga. It needs regular watering in summer; basin irrigation or drip irrigation around the palm works well. Organic manure, green manure grown in the basin and mulching with coconut husks improve the soil and hold moisture. Rhinoceros beetle, red palm weevil and the eriophyid mite are the main pests, and bud rot and stem bleeding are the main diseases.

## Arecanut: care
Arecanut is grown in Shivamogga, Dakshina Kannada, Uttara Kannada and Chikkamagaluru. It needs shade from the afternoon sun when young, regular irrigation in summer and good drainage in the rainy season. Fruit rot (koleroga) spreads in continuous heavy rain, so protective steps are taken before the monsoon on the advice of the horticulture department or CPCRI.

## Mango: care
Mango is grown in Kolar, Chikkaballapura, Ramanagara and Dharwad. Young trees need regular watering, but mature trees should not be irrigated from about two months before flowering until fruit set, because watering then promotes leaves instead of flowers. Hoppers at flowering and fruit fly at fruit ripening are the main pests; fruit fly traps and collecting and destroying fallen fruits reduce damage.

## Coffee: growing
Coffee is grown in Kodagu, Chikkamagaluru and Hassan under shade trees. Arabica grows at higher altitudes and needs cooler weather than robusta. Blossom showers in March and April bring flowering; where rain fails, sprinkler irrigation is given as a blossom shower. Leaf rust and white stem borer are the main problems. The Coffee Board gives technical advice.

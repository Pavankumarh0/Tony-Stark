# More Pests and Diseases: How to Identify and Manage Them

Review status: general guide compiled for Smart Krishi from integrated pest management (IPM) references. For chemical control, the right product and dose depend on the crop stage and the pest level: ask the agriculture or horticulture officer or the Krishi Vigyan Kendra, and follow the label. Never use banned pesticides.

## Rice: brown planthopper
Brown planthoppers are small brown insects that collect at the base of rice plants. Heavy attack makes patches of the crop dry up in circles, called hopper burn. Avoid excess nitrogen fertilizer and very close planting, drain the field for a few days when hoppers are seen, and leave gaps in the crop for air and light.

## Tomato: fruit borer and leaf miner
The tomato fruit borer caterpillar makes round holes in fruits and feeds inside. The South American leaf miner (Tuta absoluta) makes white blotches in leaves and bores into fruits. Collect and destroy damaged fruits, use pheromone traps for both pests, and grow marigold as a trap crop around tomato fields for fruit borer.

## Brinjal: shoot and fruit borer
The caterpillar bores into tender shoots, which wilt, and later into fruits. Cut and destroy wilted shoots and bored fruits every week, use pheromone traps, and avoid planting brinjal continuously in the same field.

## Cabbage and cauliflower: diamondback moth
Small green caterpillars eat the leaves, leaving holes and a window-like skin. Grow mustard rows as a trap crop, use pheromone traps, and spray neem seed kernel extract early. Repeated use of the same insecticide makes this pest resistant.

## Thrips on onion and chilli
Thrips are tiny insects that scrape leaves, leaving silvery streaks on onion and upward curling of chilli leaves. Use blue sticky traps, grow a border crop of maize or jowar, and avoid water stress. In chilli, thrips also spread leaf curl virus.

## Whitefly and mealybug
Whiteflies are tiny white insects under the leaves that suck sap and spread viruses in tomato, chilli, cotton and beans. Mealybugs are white, cottony insects on stems and fruits of cotton, papaya and many plants. Use yellow sticky traps for whitefly, remove and destroy badly infested plant parts, control ants that protect mealybugs, and encourage natural enemies such as ladybird beetles.

## Red gram and bengal gram: pod borer
The pod borer caterpillar bores into pods and eats the seeds. Install pheromone traps and bird perches, shake the plants over a cloth to collect larvae, and spray neem seed kernel extract or a biopesticide (NPV) at the early stage.

## Groundnut: leaf miner and white grub
Leaf miner larvae mine the leaves and later fold and web them. White grubs live in the soil and cut the roots, so plants wilt and die. Deep summer ploughing exposes grubs to birds, light traps after the first rains catch the beetles, and crop rotation reduces both pests.

## Sugarcane: early shoot borer
The borer attacks young cane, causing the central shoot to dry, called dead heart. Plant early, remove and destroy dead hearts, earth up and give light irrigation, and use trash mulching.

## Coconut: rhinoceros beetle and red palm weevil
The rhinoceros beetle bores into the crown and cuts the leaves in a V-shaped pattern. The red palm weevil grub bores inside the trunk; holes oozing brown liquid and a crown that falls over are signs. Keep the garden clean, destroy breeding sites in manure pits and rotting logs, avoid wounds on the trunk, and use pheromone traps. Ask the horticulture department for treatment.

## Banana: bunchy top virus
Leaves become narrow, upright and bunched at the top, with dark green streaks, and the plant gives no bunch. The virus is spread by banana aphids and through infected suckers. Uproot and destroy infected plants, control aphids, and plant only disease free suckers or tissue culture plants.

## Rats in fields and stores
Rats damage rice, ragi, groundnut, coconut and stored grain. Keep field bunds narrow and clean, close burrows, keep stores clean with grain off the floor, and use traps. Community rat control across neighbouring fields works best.

## Using neem and biopesticides
Neem seed kernel extract and neem oil repel and stop many insects, and are safer for people and natural enemies. Biopesticides such as Trichoderma and Pseudomonas for diseases, and NPV, Beauveria and Bt for caterpillars, are available from KVKs and agriculture departments. They work best when used early, before the pest becomes severe.

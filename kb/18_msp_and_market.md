# Minimum Support Prices and Selling Crops

Source: Government of India, Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs. Kharif marketing season 2026-27 prices were approved on 13 May 2026 and rabi marketing season 2027-28 prices on 30 September 2026, as reported by DD News and Business Standard. Check the latest prices at desagri.gov.in or with the procurement agency before selling. All prices are in rupees per quintal (one quintal is 100 kilograms).

## What MSP means
The Minimum Support Price (MSP) is the price at which the government agencies buy a crop from farmers when the market price falls below it. It is announced before each season on the advice of the Commission for Agricultural Costs and Prices. Selling at MSP needs registration with the state procurement agency and produce of the required quality (clean, dry and free of damage). The market (mandi) price can be above or below the MSP, so compare both before selling.

## MSP for kharif cereals, 2026-27
For the kharif marketing season 2026-27, the MSP of paddy (common) is 2441 rupees per quintal. Jowar (hybrid) is 4023 rupees, jowar (maldandi) is 4073 rupees, bajra is 2900 rupees, ragi is 5205 rupees and maize is 2410 rupees per quintal.

## MSP for kharif pulses, oilseeds and cotton, 2026-27
For the kharif marketing season 2026-27, the MSP of tur (red gram) is 8450 rupees per quintal, moong (green gram) is 8780 rupees and urad (black gram) is 8200 rupees. Groundnut is 7517 rupees per quintal. Cotton (medium staple) is 8267 rupees and cotton (long staple) is 8667 rupees per quintal.

## MSP for rabi crops, 2027-28
For the rabi marketing season 2027-28, the MSP of wheat is 2610 rupees per quintal, barley is 2286 rupees, gram (bengal gram) is 5958 rupees, lentil (masur) is 7390 rupees, rapeseed and mustard is 6613 rupees and safflower is 7215 rupees per quintal.

## Getting a better price for your crop
Clean, grade and dry the produce before taking it to the market, because graded produce fetches a better price. Check the day's prices in more than one mandi before selling; Smart Krishi can tell today's mandi prices from AGMARKNET. Selling together through a Farmer Producer Organisation (FPO) gives better bargaining power and lower transport cost. The e-NAM online market lets farmers sell to buyers in other markets. Storing non-perishable produce in a warehouse and taking a warehouse receipt loan can help avoid selling at harvest time, when prices are usually lowest.

## Crop demand and planning what to grow
Prices of vegetables such as tomato and onion swing widely, because many farmers plant at the same time after a year of high prices. Spreading planting dates, growing a mix of crops and keeping some area under crops with MSP support, such as ragi, jowar and pulses, reduce the risk. Millets and pulses are in growing demand. The local agriculture or horticulture officer can advise on crops with good demand in your area.
